Understanding Different Disabilities

Disabilities affect people in different ways. A person may need accessibility tools, communication support, extra time or help with everyday activities. Every person is unique; ask what support they prefer.

1. Locomotor Disability

May affect movement, balance or use of limbs. Some people use wheelchairs, walkers, crutches or other aids.

Helpful support: Keep pathways clear, provide ramps and ask before assisting with mobility equipment.

2. Visual Impairment

Includes blindness and low vision. People may use canes, screen readers, magnifiers or Braille.

Helpful support: Introduce yourself, describe important surroundings and ask before guiding someone.

3. Hearing Impairment

Includes deafness and difficulty hearing. People may communicate through sign language, speech, writing or hearing devices.

Helpful support: Face the person, keep your face visible and use their preferred communication method.

4. Speech and Language Disabilities

May affect speaking, understanding language or expressing ideas. Some people use picture boards or communication devices.

Helpful support: Be patient, listen without interrupting and give the person enough time to respond.

5. Intellectual Disability

Can affect learning, reasoning and everyday adaptive skills. Support needs vary from person to person.

Helpful support: Use clear language, explain one step at a time and encourage independence.

6. Autism Spectrum Disorder

Can affect social communication, sensory processing, routines and interests. Autistic people have varied strengths and support needs.

Helpful support: Respect sensory needs, explain changes clearly and do not assume everyone communicates alike.

7. Specific Learning Disabilities

May affect reading, writing or mathematics, including dyslexia, dysgraphia and dyscalculia.

Helpful support: Offer accessible learning materials, extra time where appropriate and different ways to practise.

8. Cerebral Palsy

A group of conditions affecting movement, posture or coordination. Some people may also have speech or other support needs.

Helpful support: Allow enough time for movement and communication, and speak directly to the person.

9. Multiple Sclerosis

A condition affecting the central nervous system. It may cause fatigue, mobility changes, vision problems or other symptoms.

Helpful support: Respect rest needs and avoid assuming that someone's abilities are the same every day.

10. Chronic Neurological Conditions

Some neurological conditions can affect movement, memory, sensation or daily activities in different ways.

Helpful support: Ask what assistance is useful and respect the person's choices and privacy.

11. Muscular Dystrophy

A group of genetic conditions that can cause muscle weakness. The effects and progression differ among individuals.

Helpful support: Make spaces accessible and do not move mobility equipment without permission.

12. Chronic Health Conditions

Long-term health conditions may affect energy, stamina or daily participation. Not every condition is visible.

Helpful support: Believe people when they describe their needs and avoid making assumptions based on appearance.

13. Mental Illness

Mental health conditions can affect emotions, thinking, mood or everyday life. They are not a reason to stigmatise someone.

Helpful support: Treat the person respectfully, listen without judgement and encourage appropriate support when wanted.

14. Speech-Related Communication Needs

Some people cannot rely on speech to communicate. They may use gestures, symbols, text or speech-generating technology.

Helpful support: Keep their communication tools available and give them time to express themselves.

15. Deafblindness

A combined hearing and vision disability. People use different communication and mobility methods depending on their needs.

Helpful support: Ask how to communicate and how the person prefers to be guided.

16. Thalassemia

An inherited blood disorder that affects haemoglobin. Its effects and treatment needs vary by person.

Helpful support: Respect health-related needs and never make assumptions about what someone can do.

17. Haemophilia

An inherited condition in which blood does not clot normally. People may need medical care to manage bleeding risks.

Helpful support: Respect medical advice and follow appropriate emergency guidance if an injury occurs.

18. Sickle Cell Disease

An inherited blood condition that can cause pain and other complications. Experiences vary among individuals.

Helpful support: Take reported symptoms seriously and respect the person's care plan and privacy.

19. Multiple Disabilities

A person may have more than one disability and may need a combination of communication, mobility or learning supports.

Helpful support: Ask the person or their chosen supporter what works best for them.

20. Acid Attack Survivors

Survivors may experience physical injuries, vision changes or scarring and may need accessibility or social support.

Helpful support: Respect privacy, avoid intrusive questions and focus on the person's preferences and abilities.

21. Parkinson's Disease

A neurological condition that may affect movement, balance, speech and other functions. Symptoms vary between people.

Helpful support: Allow time for movement and communication, and ask before offering physical assistance.

Important: This is an educational overview, not a diagnostic tool. India's Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 recognises 21 specified disabilities. This page is a simplified introduction and is not a substitute for the Act or official guidance. Some descriptions above are educational examples rather than the exact legal category names.

How to Be Respectful and Inclusive

Ask, Don't Assume

Ask whether someone wants help, and respect their answer. People know their own needs best.

Communicate Patiently

Give people time to respond. Speak directly to them and use their preferred communication method.

Make Spaces Accessible

Keep paths clear, provide readable information and support inclusive participation at school and in public.

Respect Privacy

Do not ask intrusive questions about someone's medical history or share personal information without permission.

Include Everyone

Make decisions with disabled people, not just about them. Include their ideas and preferences.

Recognise Individuality

A disability does not define an entire person. Everyone has preferences, skills, relationships and goals.
